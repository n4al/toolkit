Model & Capacity Audit

What capacities does an explanation assume, use or leave to be explained?

This worksheet examines the capacities involved in a published model, proposed intervention or empirical case. It distinguishes what a source establishes from what a researcher infers or proposes to investigate.

The audit does not assume that relinking is necessary, feasible or sufficient. It can identify capacities that are absent, externally controlled, reproduced through existing relationships or potentially developed through alternative arrangements.

What this instrument does

Organises source reconstruction, capacity assumptions, relationships and evidence gaps into a reusable research record. It does not calculate a capacity score, establish causation or demonstrate that a proposed transformation will succeed.

01 / Research worksheet

Conduct an audit

Enter a published study, model, intervention or empirical case. Use "not specified" where the source does not provide an answer. Distinguish observed evidence from your own hypotheses.

Identify the precise source, version or empirical setting.

Distinguish an explicit mechanism from a relationship you infer or want to investigate.

Consider productive, technical, financial, institutional, organisational and political capacities where relevant. Do not assume every category applies.

Label each claim: demonstrated, asserted, hypothetical or not established.

An omission from one article is not necessarily an omission from an author's wider body of work.

02 / Worked example

Auditing Amin (1987)

A Note on the Concept of Delinking

This example reconstructs selected features of Samir Amin's 1987 article and identifies further questions generated by the audit. It is not an independent reproduction of his model.

Read the Methods Library profile →

What the article specifies

  • Delinking concerns the subordination of external economic relations to nationally grounded, popular development priorities; it is not equated with autarky.
  • An illustrative comparison considers national-popular and world-capitalist development strategies for an initially identical economy over ten years.
  • The scenarios share specified population-growth, productivity and investment assumptions.
  • The comparison illustrates contrasting sectoral and distributional outcomes. It is not an empirical estimate of unequal exchange.

Capacity questions raised by the audit

  • Assumed: What productive, investment, organisational and political capacities would be required to establish the alternative development priorities?
  • Exercised: Which capacities are involved in reorganising investment, production and distribution?
  • Produced: Under what conditions might altered economic relationships generate further technical, institutional or collective capacities?
  • Control: Who can direct investment, coordinate production and determine the terms of external economic relationships?
  • Reproduction: What would sustain those capacities across changing economic and political conditions?

What remains unresolved

The 1987 note refers readers to Amin's earlier work for detailed technical arguments. The illustrative comparison does not independently establish how all the capacities required to undertake and sustain such a transformation would be formed.

These are questions prompted by the audit, not a claim that Amin's wider work ignores political organisation, internal articulation or the processual character of delinking.

Source: Samir Amin (1987), "A Note on the Concept of Delinking," Review (Fernand Braudel Center), 10(3), pp. 435–444. See the linked Methods Library profile for source documentation and verification status.

03 / Methodological status

What this audit cannot establish

Completing a worksheet does not demonstrate that a capacity exists, that a relationship causes its development or that reorganising an economy would produce autonomy. Those claims require appropriate empirical and historical evidence.

The audit is designed to make assumptions, mechanisms, evidentiary requirements and unresolved questions visible. Its categories are analytical prompts, not a scoring system or a predetermined sequence of development.
