Experimental research / Prototype 01

Relinking Lab

What does changing an economic relationship make possible?

The Relinking Lab explores how reorganising relationships between producers, suppliers and institutions might enable specific capacities to emerge, become accessible and reproduce over time.

Rather than assuming that a new partnership creates autonomy, the Lab asks what functions become possible, who controls them, what they depend upon and whether they can be sustained.

What this prototype does

It generates a research specification from a hypothetical scenario. It identifies assumptions, questions and evidence requirements. It does not simulate economic outcomes, calculate capacity scores or establish that an intervention would succeed.

01 / The scenario

Maintenance and technical learning

Consider a hypothetical palm-oil processor in Sabah exploring a new arrangement for machinery maintenance. The proposed arrangement could connect the processor with a machinery supplier and a training institution.

The actors are illustrative. No existing partnership, shortage of skills or pattern of dependence is assumed.

Actor A

Processor

Operates machinery requiring maintenance. Its actual equipment, skills and maintenance arrangements are unspecified.

Actor B

Machinery supplier

A potential source of technical knowledge, equipment, components or support. No commitment is assumed.

Actor C

Training institution

A potential provider of instruction and skills assessment. Its actual capabilities are unknown.

Proposed intervention: Establish a maintenance arrangement connecting these actors, with technical training as a specified condition.

The existing arrangement would need to be documented before this scenario could be used in an empirical study.

02 / Explore

Build a research scenario

Select a function and specify the conditions under which it might be developed. Your selections describe a hypothetical arrangement, not observed conditions.

Scenario conditions

An empirical study would need to identify the actual machine, task and performance standard.

Training provision does not itself establish that a skill has been acquired.

Consider manuals, diagnostic tools, software, licences and permission to teach others.

Component availability, compatibility and cost require separate investigation.

A skill may be demonstrated once without the arrangements needed to reproduce it.

Different observation points reveal different questions about retention and reproduction.

      03 / Research logic

      What is the Lab investigating?

      A new relationship does not automatically produce a new capacity. The Lab distinguishes three analytical questions that would need to be investigated with evidence.

      Function

      What specific activity becomes possible? Who can perform it, using which tools and to what standard?

      Control

      Who can access, use and direct the relevant knowledge, equipment and resources? What permissions or relationships remain necessary?

      Reproduction

      Can the function be retained, renewed and passed on over time? What happens when personnel, funding or supplier relationships change?

      A central distinction

      A technician may acquire a maintenance skill without the processor gaining control over the equipment, components or arrangements needed to deploy it. The processor may also perform a repair once without being able to reproduce that capability after staff turnover.

      These possibilities must be investigated separately.

      04 / Methodological note

      Status and limitations

      This is an experimental research-specification tool. It uses a hypothetical scenario to organise questions about relational change and the production and reproduction of specific capacities.

      The current prototype does not use an empirical dataset, estimate causal effects, simulate economic behaviour, assess the feasibility of a real project or measure unequal exchange.

      Its outputs depend on user-selected assumptions. Options such as "local provision demonstrated" describe conditions a user may choose to investigate; selecting them does not supply evidence that those conditions exist.

      An empirical application would require a defined machine and maintenance task, documentation of existing arrangements, evidence concerning participating actors, observable performance standards and a strategy for investigating change over time and alternative explanations.

      The relationship between relinking and unequal exchange is a further research question. A change in maintenance arrangements cannot, by itself, establish that unequal exchange has been reduced.

      Version 0.1 · Conceptual prototype · No empirical results. The scenario is illustrative and should not be interpreted as a description of any particular Sabah enterprise or institution.
