Experimental research / 03

Relinking Lab

What happens when economic relationships are reorganised so that existing and emerging capacities can support, extend and reproduce one another?

The Relinking Lab is a proposed modelling environment for investigating these processes through explicit scenarios, relationships and capacity requirements.

The research problem

From dependency to the production of capacity

Analysing dependency can help identify constraints within existing economic relationships. But identifying a dependency does not, by itself, explain how the capacities required to transform that relationship might be developed.

The Relinking Lab investigates a different question: how might changing relationships among producers, workers, institutions, technologies and other resources generate new capacities, and how might those capacities enable further changes?

Relinking is treated here as a potentially iterative process. Changes in relationships may produce capacities that make additional transformations possible. Whether this occurs must be investigated rather than assumed.

Modelling principles

What the model needs to distinguish

01 / Relationships

Who is connected to whom?

Map the relevant economic and institutional relationships, including supply, ownership, finance, training and technical support where applicable.

02 / Functions

What can each actor do?

Specify capacities in terms of concrete functions, rather than assigning an unexplained aggregate capacity score.

03 / Control

Who can access and direct them?

Distinguish the existence of a resource or capability from access to it, control over it and the conditions governing its use.

04 / Reproduction

Can capacities be sustained?

Investigate whether a proposed arrangement can maintain, repair, replenish and extend the capacities it requires over time.

Illustrative scenario

A hypothetical Sabah production network

An initial scenario could examine relationships among a palm-oil processor, a machinery supplier and a training institution in Sabah.

The model could ask what changes if these actors develop new arrangements for equipment supply, maintenance, technical learning and local production.

The relevant outcomes would need to be specified individually: for example, the ability to maintain a particular machine, produce a component or train additional technicians.

This is a hypothetical research scenario, not a description of an observed partnership or a prediction of its effects. No numerical outcomes have been calculated.

Research design

From scenarios to testable models

A future implementation would need to identify its actors, relationships, starting conditions, proposed interventions, required resources and measurable outcomes.

Assumptions should be visible and adjustable. Hypothetical inputs must remain clearly distinguishable from observed data, and any calculations must be documented before their results are presented as meaningful evidence.

The Relinking Lab is not itself an unequal-exchange calculator. It addresses the production and reproduction of capacities for transformation, although its scenarios may draw on evidence generated elsewhere in the toolkit.
