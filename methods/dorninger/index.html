What this method establishes — and what it does not

Dorninger et al. combine environmentally extended multiregional input–output analysis, value-added measures and structural equation modelling. These components answer different questions and should not be treated as interchangeable evidence.

What the method can investigate

  • Embodied resource flows: the distribution of materials, energy, land and labour embodied in international production and consumption.
  • Physical and monetary asymmetries: relationships between countries' embodied-resource trade positions and monetary returns.
  • Statistical associations: relationships among the variables included in the specified structural equation model.

Limits of inference

Net physical transfers do not automatically establish monetary value transfers. Differences in monetary returns do not, by themselves, identify a unique causal mechanism. A satisfactory structural equation model fit does not establish that its specified relationships are causal.

The 2015 country-level model also cannot, on its own, establish historical trajectories or identify how benefits and costs are distributed among actors within countries.

Independent reconstruction

The Toolkit independently reconstructed the 2015 materials structural equation model using the publisher's supplementary data. After correcting an extra relationship in the initial implementation, the R model produced Fisher's C = 2.531 (p = 0.639), closely matching the published Fisher's C = 2.57 (p = 0.63).

This is close agreement in global model fit, not verification of every coefficient or of the upstream input–output calculations. Exact replication remains unresolved.

Questions for further research

Further work could examine sensitivity to the underlying input–output database, differences in sectoral composition, competing explanations for monetary asymmetries, changes over time and distributional outcomes within countries. Investigating how these relationships might be transformed requires additional evidence about productive, institutional and political capacities.
