METHODS LIBRARY / RESEARCH PROFILE 001

Dorninger et al. (2021): Global patterns of ecologically unequal exchange

A documented account of how one study traces embodied resources, trade in value added and statistical relationships across the world economy.

Partial independent reconstruction — verification ongoing

Primary source. Dorninger, C., Hornborg, A., Abson, D. J., et al. (2021). “Global patterns of ecologically unequal exchange: Implications for sustainability in the 21st century.” Ecological Economics 179, 106824. DOI / publisher page. This profile is an independent educational reconstruction, not author-endorsed code.

1. Research question and scope

The authors examine international asymmetries in embodied materials, energy, land and labour, alongside monetary value added, over 1990–2015. Their global database covers 189 countries, while their reported analytical sample covers 170 countries. For comparisons they classify countries into income-based groups, treating China and India separately. These categories are specific to this study.

3. Core accounting logic

Input–output transactions yield a direct-input coefficient matrix A and Leontief inverse L = (I − A)⁻¹. With final demand y, physical extensions e, output x and value added v, the paper defines physical intensity q = e / x and value-added intensity p = v / x. The corresponding footprint matrices are F = q̂Lŷ and B = p̂Lŷ, where hats indicate diagonal matrices. These accounting matrices allocate upstream physical inputs and value added to final demand. See the published equations in sections 3.1–3.2; this is a conceptual transcription, not executable Eora code.

4. Statistical model: materials

Figure 4a depicts five component regressions involving reserves, income, technological adoption, military expenditure, net imports of raw material equivalents and value added per exported material unit. The paper specifies linear models for technology and net imports, and Gamma models with log links for income, military expenditure and value-added outcomes. The authors used R and piecewiseSEM. Their reported overall materials-model fit is Fisher’s C = 2.57, p = 0.63.

6. Independent reconstruction

The accompanying R script is an independently authored attempt to reconstruct the five materials regressions and a piecewiseSEM model from the publisher’s 2015 supplementary spreadsheet. Download the spreadsheet from the publisher page and follow the script’s instructions. The script has not yet been executed and verified in R. Do not interpret it as the authors’ original code or as a successful replication.

7. Interpretation and limitations

The study distinguishes asymmetric physical transfers from a claim that commodities possess an objectively measurable value above their market price. Its country-level design cannot resolve within-country appropriation or historical emergence; the authors also identify limits in their technological-infrastructure proxy and sensitivity of EEMRIO estimates to database construction and environmental extensions. Statistical associations should not be read as proof of a single causal mechanism.

8. Connection to the Relinking Lab

The paper investigates how resource transfers and value-added patterns relate to the distribution of productive infrastructure. The Relinking Lab asks a distinct, additional question: how might particular productive, technical and organisational capacities be assembled and reproduced through changing relationships? This is a proposed research extension, not a finding derived from the paper’s model.
