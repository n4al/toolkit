How much growth is required to achieve good lives for all?

Jason Hickel and Dylan Sullivan (2024). “How much growth is required to achieve good lives for all? Insights from needs-based analysis.” World Development Perspectives 35, article 100612. DOI: 10.1016/j.wdp.2024.100612 .

Scope: This is a needs-based development and resource-requirements analysis. It cites unequal-exchange research but does not itself reproduce the 2022 unequal-exchange accounting method or generate a new estimate of net appropriation.

Research question and argument

The authors ask whether universal decent living requires all countries to converge on high-income-country GDP per capita, and instead examine the production and resource requirements of meeting specific needs. They argue that the composition of production and access through public provisioning matter more than aggregate GDP alone. This profile documents the authors' argument rather than independently endorsing its policy prescriptions.

Analytical and estimation strategy

  1. Contrast GDP/income-convergence scenarios with needs-based poverty measures, including the basic needs poverty line (BNPL).
  2. Use published decent living standards (DLS) specifications to define necessary goods and services.
  3. Draw on published energy-requirement scenarios from Millward-Hopkins (2022) and material-requirement scenarios from Vélez-Henao and Pauliuk (2023).
  4. Scale per-capita requirements to a projected population of 8.5 billion in 2050, compare with reported 2019 global energy and material use, and consider alternative technology, consumption and inequality assumptions.

The article synthesises and adapts published estimates; it is not a new multiregional input–output unequal-exchange calculation.

Data and units

Needs-based poverty
Allen (2020) for the 2011 BNPL population-weighted estimate; Moatsos (2021) for historical basic-needs poverty and local-price comparisons. Historical GDP-per-capita examples use Bolt and van Zanden (2020).
Energy
Millward-Hopkins (2022) DLS requirements; 2019 global energy-use comparison of 418 EJ, attributed to IEA (2021).
Materials
Vélez-Henao and Pauliuk (2023) scenarios; 2019 global material-use comparison of 95 Gt, attributed to UNEP International Resource Panel.
Population
Projected 8.5 billion people in 2050, consistent with SSP1 as used in the cited DLS analysis.
Unequal-exchange evidence
Hickel et al. (2022) is cited for the 43% net-appropriation figure. The 2024 article does not identify the 2022 MRIO dataset as its own DLS scenario dataset.

Published quantities and scenario assumptions

The paper reports DLS energy requirements of approximately 125–183 EJ annually (30–44% of reported 2019 global energy use), depending on technology. Its materials scenarios require approximately 28–40 Gt annually (29–42% of reported 2019 global material use). These are scenario comparisons and depend on the cited models, population projection and assumptions about technologies, diets, housing and transport; they are not forecasts or independently replicated Toolkit results.

Figure 2 (p. 6) compares energy scenarios; Figure 3 (p. 6) compares material scenarios. Table 1 (p. 5) describes DLS dimensions and minimum activity levels.

Limitations and verification

Toolkit status: Source-based documentation; replication not attempted. The Toolkit has not independently obtained, reconciled or rerun the underlying DLS datasets and model code. Exact dataset versions, transformations and scenario reproduction remain to be checked against the underlying studies.

The 2024 paper explicitly cautions that historical BNPL estimates outside periods with direct price and survey data involve proxies or assumptions (p. 3, footnote 3). Its 2050 scenarios are conditional comparisons rather than an estimate of the GDP path needed to implement the transition; the authors say that would require further modelling (p. 6).

Connections to the library
